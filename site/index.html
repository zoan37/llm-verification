llm-verification · black-box audit

Is each provider serving the model it lists?

Every OpenRouter endpoint of a model got the same prompts. A referee running the real weights replayed each reply and measured how well it fits. Rows sit on a scale from an honest machine running those weights (100) to a known substitute (0).

no swaps

No endpoint looks like a different model. Most serve something consistent with the claimed weights; on Gemma-4 three are indistinguishable from honest BF16 serving.

lossy

A meaningful minority sit at or past a 3- to 4-bit copy's distance (Qwen3.5: AtlasCloud, Darkbloom, Alibaba; Gemma-4: Darkbloom, AkashML). Distance, not proof of intent.

silent defaults

Asked for temperature 1 and top_p 1, six of seven Qwen3.5 endpoints almost never sample long-tail tokens: an unrequested top-k, most likely.

broken outputs

Misaligned logprobs (DekaLLM, Wafer on Qwen3.8), dropped tokens (DekaLLM on gpt-oss), and prompts that differ from the published template (several gpt-oss hosts).

When the provider opts in

Providers that run the llmverify server sign every reply (exact prompt tokens, output tokens, top-20 logprobs, sampled with a seed the client picks). Measured on real weights: Qwen3.5-35B-A3B as the honest provider, cheats built from Qwen3.5-4B, 16 replies of 128 tokens per variant at temperature 0 and 0.7, thresholds at a 0.1% false-flag rate on honest cross-hardware noise.

What each column means

Shaded rows: references

Our own runs, scored exactly like the endpoints. Honest rows run the real weights faithfully on other hardware (or a near-lossless copy): they show how close "really the model" gets. Substitute rows are deliberately wrong (a heavily quantized copy, or a smaller model posing as the big one): they show what clearly-not-the-model looks like.

Fidelity

Where an endpoint sits between the two references: 100 = as close to the real model as the honest reference, 0 = as far as the substitute. Measured on log distance and clipped to 0-100. A ranking aid; the columns after it are the evidence.

Verdict

consistent (close to the real model), lossy (measurably further than honest serving), far off (as far as the substitute or further), broken output (model looks fine but what it returns is wrong), check (an outlier or missing data). Distance, not intent.

Regret

At temperature 0 the referee (the real model) replays every reply and asks: how much less likely was the endpoint's token than the real model's own first choice? In nats per token; 0 when they match. Bracket: 95% interval. Bar: log scale with ticks at the honest and substitute levels.

× honest

Regret divided by the honest reference's regret. 1× = as close as an honest second machine; 5× = five times further off.

Other token

Share of temperature-0 tokens where the endpoint picked something other than the real model's favourite. Honest machines are around 1-2% (near-ties flip on different hardware). "big/1k" counts serious misses: tokens the real model rated at least 10× less likely than its favourite, per 1,000 tokens.

Sampled fit (PIT)

At temperature 1, where each sampled token falls in the real model's distribution: 0.500 on average for true samples, with no calibration needed. Above 0.5 means it picks less likely tokens than the real model would. * marks p < 0.001. "tail" counts long-tail tokens (p < 1e-4) observed vs expected: near 1× is honest sampling, near 0× means a silent top-k, well above 1× a flatter substitute.

Peer agreement

How long an endpoint's temperature-0 replies stay word-for-word identical to the other endpoints' (first 200 characters, median over peers). Needs no reference weights, but rewards being like the crowd, not being correct: only far-below-everyone values mean much.

Same reply at T=0

Share of repeated temperature-0 prompts that came back identical. Most servers batch requests, which changes the arithmetic slightly, so below 100% is normal.

Sampled at T=0 / Self-PIT / Misaligned logprobs

Checks on the endpoint's own reported logprobs (no referee): picking tokens that are not its own top choice when asked for greedy; whether its probabilities explain its own temperature-1 samples (0.500 = yes); and whether its top-20 lists even belong to the token it returned.

Prompt tokens

The prompt size the endpoint billed. Everyone running the published template bills the same; a different number means it added to or changed your prompt.

Flags

Specific problems in what an endpoint returns: dropped tokens, edited prompts, broken logprobs, undisclosed sampling defaults, failed requests.

A low score is not proof of cheating. Honest serving choices (FP8 weights, an FP8 KV cache, different kernels) move outputs too, and a provider can behave differently for audit traffic than for yours. A high score means the replies we got are statistically consistent with the real model at this sample size. What the checks can and cannot prove is in docs/soundness.md.
